## When do you need a commercial license?

You need a commercial license if **any** of the following apply:

- You integrate Apiary into a **proprietary product or service** and do not wish to release your source code under AGPLv3.
- You offer Apiary (or a modified version of it) as a **hosted/SaaS service** to third parties.
- You build **enterprise features or plugins** on top of Apiary without open-sourcing them.
- Your organization has a **policy against AGPLv3** software in your stack.

## What the commercial license gives you

- Full rights to use, modify, and distribute Apiary in proprietary products without AGPLv3 obligations.
- Access to **Apiary Enterprise** — closed-source features built on top of the open-source core (roadmap).
- Priority support and SLA options (roadmap).
- License key mechanism to unlock enterprise-only capabilities.

## What is always free

- Using Apiary for **personal projects**.
- Using Apiary in **open-source projects** that are themselves licensed under AGPLv3-compatible terms.
- **Contributing** to Apiary — all contributions are welcome and remain under AGPLv3.
- **Self-hosting** Apiary for internal use at your company, as long as you do not distribute it externally or build proprietary products on top of it.

Commercial licensing is not yet available (Apiary is in early development). To express interest or get notified when commercial licensing opens:

## Contributor License Agreement (CLA)

All contributors to Apiary must sign a Contributor License Agreement (CLA) before their pull request can be merged. The CLA grants the project maintainer the right to re-license contributions for commercial purposes, while preserving your rights as the author under AGPLv3.

> A CLA bot will be added to the repository to manage this automatically.

## FAQ

**Can I use Apiary internally at my company without a commercial license?**
Yes, internal use without distributing or offering it as a service is permitted under AGPLv3.

**Do I need a commercial license to build a Plane or Jira integration?**
No — you can write a custom source adapter for internal use under AGPLv3. You only need a commercial license if you distribute that adapter as a proprietary product.

**Is there a free tier for startups or small teams?**
This is being considered. Follow the repository for updates.
